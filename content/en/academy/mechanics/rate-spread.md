A rate spread is the price gap of one coin on two exchanges. On the cheap one you open a long, on the
dear one a short; the profit comes from CONVERGENCE of the prices, not from their movement.

## What it looks like

Two price charts of one coin on two exchanges run apart, then draw together. The profit is the shrinking
distance between them. Where the market goes overall — up or down — does not matter: both legs move
together, and you live on the difference.

## Anti-spread

A spread can not only shrink but widen further — then the position is temporarily in the red. That is
normal while the spread has a market reason to converge. The danger is different — when there is nothing
to converge to (closed withdrawal, different indices); that is covered in the risk section.

## The takeaway

A rate spread is a bet not on a rise or a fall but on two prices of one coin coming together. The main
question before entering is whether they have a reason to converge.
