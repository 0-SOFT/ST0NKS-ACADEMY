The gap between gross and net return in arbitrage is, in one study's words, "brutal, and beginners
systematically underrate it."

## The break-even threshold

A typical two-sided trade on a $100,000 position costs **$200–205** at standard fees (0.1% maker + 0.1%
taker + ~$5 withdrawal) and **$100–120** with VIP tiers and discounts. That sets a minimum gross spread
for break-even of **0.3–0.5%** (with a margin for slippage). Anything below is a loss even on perfect
execution.

## Fee optimization is part of the math, not a convenience

Exchange tokens cut fees (on the order of 25–50%), and some venues offer a zero maker fee. A zero maker
turns a venue into a "useful leg" for many pairs: the choice of exchange is part of the profit
calculation, not just a question of comfort.

## Cross-network transfer and taxes

The cost and time of moving between networks often decide whether anything is left of a spread: gas on
an expensive network eats a thin gap whole. And the profit that does remain is taxable in most
jurisdictions — keep a record of trades from day one; reconstructing it later costs more than the tax.

## The takeaway

A spread is a gross figure. Between it and money in the account stand fees, slippage, gas and tax — and
it is those, not the size of the spread, that decide whether a trade was profitable.
