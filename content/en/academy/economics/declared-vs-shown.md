The same number, "30–60%," is quoted both by honest research and by service advertising. The difference
is one word, and it is the first thing to look at.

## The swap itself

| | Period | What it is per year |
|---|---|---|
| "30–60% **a month**" | month | **2,230–28,047% a year** |
| "30–60% **a year**" | year | 30–60% |

The math is simple: 1.30 to the 12th is a 23-fold rise, 1.60 to the 12th is 280-fold. The gap between
two readings of one number is thirty to four hundred times. A monthly "30–60%" is beyond not only
reality but the theoretical maximum that studies place at 80–145% a year under ideal conditions.

## How to read any return figure

1. Over what PERIOD? "30%" a month and a year are different universes.
2. GROSS or NET? Before fees and withdrawal, or after.
3. Where is the figure from? An independent measurement, or someone selling access.

## The takeaway

A return figure with no unit is not a fact but an impression. When you meet "30–60%," look first not at
the number but at the word next to it.
