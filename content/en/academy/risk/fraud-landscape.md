A separate article covers the schemes themselves. This one gives the scale in numbers from independent
sources, so it is clear that the "arbitrage" bait is not a rarity but an industry.

## The largest documented category

Fake "arbitrage bot" smart contracts promoted through video. An academic study (ACM SIGMETRICS, 2023)
counted more than **10,000 fraudulent videos** in a single year.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Documented losses | over **$15M** |
| Victims | **25,000+** |
| One wallet's take from one video | **$902,000** |

The mechanism is always the same: the video instructs the viewer to deploy a Solidity contract and
deposit ETH "to fund the bot"; the contract hides the attacker's address, where all funds go. It works
because the victim acts THEMSELVES, following a "technical" instruction — the feeling of control is the
bait.

## The scale in messengers

Independent estimates describe dozens of cloned trading platforms run by organized groups, hundreds of
thousands of pump-and-dump messages over several years, and a sharp rise in malware-based attacks. The
exact figures differ by source, but the order of magnitude is the same: not isolated cases, a flow.

## The filter that works

Behind all this variety is the same tell as in the individual schemes: the victim sends funds FIRST.
The numbers change, the videos and platforms change, but that step is always there — and it is what
separates a trap from real work.

## The takeaway

The scale is here not to frighten but to remove the feeling of "this won't happen to me." It happens to
tens of thousands, and almost all of them, at the moment of the transfer, were sure they were in control.
