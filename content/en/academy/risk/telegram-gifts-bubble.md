Next to arbitrage there are always markets that look like it but are built differently. One example is
collectible Telegram gifts resold for TON. The price there rests not on a gap between venues but on
scarcity and fashion — that is, on the expectation that someone will buy higher.

## Why this is in the risk section

An arbitrage price gap converges because a market reason holds it. A collectible's price has nothing to
converge to: there is no "fair" level it must return to. The very sources that describe such markets
call them a bubble and warn that far from all items rise, and mindless buying easily leaves you with
nothing.

## How to tell it from real work

- In arbitrage the gap converges; in a bubble the price rests on the next buyer showing up.
- A collectible market's liquidity vanishes exactly when you want to exit.
- "Up two to four times in a short time" describes a risk, not an opportunity: they fall just as fast.

## The takeaway

If a "pair" rests on someone buying higher rather than on a price gap between venues, it is not
arbitrage — it is a bet on fashion. Fashion ends without warning.
