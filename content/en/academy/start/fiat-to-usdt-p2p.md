**P2P** is trading directly between users. Some post ads, others accept them; the exchange acts as guarantor and holds the crypto in escrow until both sides meet their obligations. It is the main way to buy crypto for fiat. P2P needs at least Tier 1 verification.

## The rate depends on the amount

Ads are posted by real people, and larger deals suit them better. So the smaller the amount, the worse the rate — in both directions. On small sums the gap between buy and sell reaches several percent, more than a typical arbitrage spread. Splitting your funding into small deals is not worth it.

## How to buy

1. Open the P2P trading section.
2. Set the fiat currency, amount and payment method. Turn on the verified-sellers filter.
3. Pick an ad. Look at the seller's completed-deal count and rate — the higher, the safer.
4. Confirm the purchase and pay to the seller's details within the allotted time.
5. After paying, mark the payment done. When the seller confirms, the crypto arrives in your account.

## The main safety rule

Check the seller's name against the card holder's name before paying. If they differ — ask the seller and do not send money. A mismatch between the payer and the counterparty is the main sign of a scam. Never release crypto on a screenshot of a transfer alone: confirm the money actually arrived in your bank.

## Common scams

| Scheme | What happens | Defense |
|---|---|---|
| "Triangle" | a stranger pays you using someone else's details; the claims later come to you | accept payment only from the deal's counterparty |
| Fake support | a clone of support asks for a login, password or 2FA code | support never messages first; find contacts only on the exchange site |
| Fake receipt | a forged SMS or receipt of a deposit | verify the real arrival, do not release crypto on a screenshot |
| Rate manipulation | the good rate in the ad changes mid-deal | check the rate in the ad against the deal details |

With any problem, open a dispute on the order — the exchange as guarantor will resolve it.
