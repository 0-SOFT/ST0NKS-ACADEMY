The **spot market** is the exchange's built-in converter. An asset is bought or sold directly, and the deal executes as soon as the order is placed.

## Two order types

| Type | How it works | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Market | executes at once at the current price, you set only the amount | you need to enter or exit fast and the price suits you |
| Limit | executes when the price reaches the level you set | the price does not suit you and you can wait; the book is empty or thin |

## The order book and liquidity

**Liquidity** is the amount of an asset you can trade without noticeably moving the price. The order book shows it directly.

- Many orders on both sides, prices close together — a market order fills near the expected price.
- Few orders or widely spread prices — a market order overpays on a buy and underdelivers on a sell. Here you need a limit order, or come back later.

The gap between the expected and received price is called **slippage**. On illiquid pairs it eats more than the fee, and it is the first place a beginner loses money without noticing.

## If you have funds but do not see them when buying

On many exchanges money sits in separate accounts — a funding account and a trading account, say. To trade, move it to the trading account: open a transfer, set the amount and direction, confirm.

## The order of a trade

1. Open the spot section and pick a pair.
2. Choose the order type — market or limit.
3. Read the book, set the price (for a limit order) and amount, confirm.

Selling mirrors this.
