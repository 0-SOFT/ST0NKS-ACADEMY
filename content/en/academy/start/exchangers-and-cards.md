Besides P2P there are two more ways to get crypto. Both are worse than P2P on rate, but each has a reason to exist.

## Third-party exchangers

These suit people who would rather not do exchange verification. The rate is found through aggregator-monitors of exchange points, which compare dozens of services by conversion direction.

The order of a purchase:

1. Pick the direction — say, paying by card and receiving USDT on the needed network. Sort by rate.
2. Go to the chosen exchanger's site and check the payment method and asset.
3. Enter the amount and check the conversion. Watch the fee: on a small sum it can eat the whole rate advantage you picked the exchanger for.
4. Enter your wallet address and check the network carefully.

> The wrong network means lost funds: the transfer cannot be cancelled and, as a rule, cannot be returned. It is one of the most common and expensive beginner mistakes.

An important point: an exchanger has no guarantor, unlike the exchange in P2P. The responsibility for picking a reliable point is yours.

## Buying with a bank card

Exchanges let you buy crypto straight from a card, but the rate here is the worst of all methods: poor conversion and high fees. The order is usually: choose the fiat currency and amount, pay to the details, then convert the credited fiat into crypto inside the exchange. The method wins on speed but loses on price.

## Comparison

| Method | Rate | Verification | Guarantor |
|---|---|---|---|
| P2P | best | KYC needed | the exchange |
| Exchanger | depends on the point, a fee applies | not required | none |
| Card | worst | KYC needed | the exchange |
