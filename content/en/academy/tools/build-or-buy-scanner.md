The question that occurs to everyone who can program: why pay for a scanner when you can write one. The answer turns out to be economic, not technical.

## What building your own costs

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Building a mid-level system | $8,000–25,000 of development time |
| Time | 2–5 months for one developer |
| Infrastructure | $300–700/mo for cloud with managed databases |
| Premium data feeds | +$300/mo |

## The tipping point — capital size

At $50k of capital, spending $10k+ on your own infrastructure eats 20%+ of your trading capital, on months of preparation with zero guarantee of profit. At $250k+ the economics change — infrastructure costs become a rounding error.

| Capital | What is wiser |
|---|---|
| $10–50k | a ready tool |
| $250k+ | your own system tuned to a specific edge |

## The main takeaway: build the telescope before the rocket

The technical side is well studied and mostly open: a competent developer stands up working monitoring in 2–3 weeks. The problem is deeper: cross-exchange spreads on major pairs in 2026 are 0.05–0.20%, while the minimum break-even cost is 0.30–0.80%. The most valuable use of technical skill is not a trading bot but a monitoring platform that surfaces market inefficiencies. Find a genuine edge first — and only then build an execution layer.

ST0NKS is exactly that monitoring platform, and it is free. You do not need to spend months and thousands of dollars to see the market: the telescope is already built and open to you.
