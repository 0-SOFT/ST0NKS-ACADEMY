A cross-network spread held at **20% for about 5–6 hours at any size**. The reason it stayed open so
long is the same reason it could not be used: no bridge between the networks existed.

## The situation

After listing, the coin traded in two places: a common network and the project's own blockchain. The
phrase "at any size" is the key: normally a spread collapses as soon as money enters it. Here it did
not collapse for hours.

## Why the spread would not close

To close a cross-network spread you buy the coin where it is cheaper and move it to where it is more
expensive. Moving between different networks needs a bridge. Here none of the known bridge services
supported the project's own network — so there was no way to move the coin, and the spread stayed a
paper one.

## The takeaway

A spread that holds for hours at any size is not a gift but a question: why has no one closed it? Most
often the answer is that there is no physical path between the legs — a bridge, a withdrawal, or a shared
network.
