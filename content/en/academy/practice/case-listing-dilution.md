An exchange announced an increase in the token count. The market cap does not change with it — so the
price should fall by exactly the dilution fraction. The trade looks arithmetically predetermined, and
that is exactly what should make you cautious.

## The mechanics

A token's price is the market cap divided by the circulating supply. If supply grows while cap stays
the same, the price falls proportionally. This is not a forecast but a consequence of the definition.
Hence the temptation: it seems enough to short before the event and close after.

## Where the trap is

On the stream, the announced numbers did not match the estimate of the effect: going from nearly 12
billion to 13 billion is about 8% supply growth, while the effect was estimated at "3–4%." When the
arithmetic and the estimate diverge, one of them is right — either not the whole issue is circulating,
or part of the dilution is already in the price. On top of that, everyone who read the same announcement
sees the same trade.

## The takeaway

An "arithmetically obvious" trade must add up in the numbers. If the announced effect does not match the
calculation, the obviousness is illusory and the risk is real.
